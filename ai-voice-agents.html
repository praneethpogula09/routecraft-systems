Designed to handle

Routine intake and the next administrative step.

  • Disclose that the caller is speaking with an AI assistant
  • Collect contact, address, job type, and urgency details
  • Answer only approved business FAQs
  • Check service-area or request-type fit using documented rules
  • Request or book an appointment where supported
  • Send a structured call summary
  • Trigger an approved inbound-workflow SMS
  • Transfer, take a message, or create a callback request

Not designed to handle

Safety judgment or promises only your team can make.

  • Pretending to be a human
  • Diagnosing electrical, gas, HVAC, plumbing, or other safety issues
  • Replacing 911 or local emergency services
  • Making unapproved pricing or arrival-time commitments
  • Collecting unnecessary sensitive information
  • Binding estimates, payments, identity verification, or professional advice in the base scope
  • Cold consumer robocalling or purchased-list outreach
  • Continuing when the approved workflow requires escalation

Control model

The team decides where automation ends.

Each workflow starts with explicit business rules. Unknown, prohibited, urgent, or sensitive situations follow an approved refusal or human fallback path.

Approved knowledge

Responses are limited to business information and FAQs your team reviews before launch.

Scripted evaluation

Normal, edge, refusal, transfer, and failure scenarios are tested before live calls.

Exception review

Monthly review focuses on failures and unclear calls—not vanity totals.

AI, recording & safety disclosure

Disclosure happens in the call—not just on this page.

RouteCraft workflows are designed to identify the assistant as AI. If recording or transcription is enabled, the client must approve an audible notice and consent flow appropriate to every relevant jurisdiction.

Illustrative opening

“Thanks for calling [Business Name]. I’m an AI assistant helping with initial call intake. I can collect a few details and help with the next step. If you prefer a person, please say ‘representative.’”

If recording or transcription is enabled

“This call may be recorded or transcribed for service, quality, and training purposes. By continuing, you acknowledge this notice. You may ask for another contact option.”

Important: This language is illustrative, not legal advice. Consent rules vary by jurisdiction and participant location. Some jurisdictions require consent from all participants. Clients remain responsible for obtaining legal review, approving the final wording, maintaining a lawful alternative, and using recordings/transcripts only for approved purposes.

AI output can be incomplete, delayed, or incorrect. Voice recognition may be affected by accents, noise, connectivity, caller behavior, and upstream services. For fire, gas odor, electrical danger, flooding, injury, or immediate threats, callers should contact 911 or the appropriate local emergency service.
